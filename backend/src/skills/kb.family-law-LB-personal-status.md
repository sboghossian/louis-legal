# Lebanon Personal Status Law — Quick Reference

## Unique Lebanese system
Lebanon has **no unified civil personal status law for Lebanese nationals**. Eighteen confessional courts each apply their own rules for:
- Marriage
- Divorce
- Custody
- Inheritance
- Adoption
- Guardianship

## Recognized confessions (with own personal-status court)
1. Maronite (Catholic)
2. Greek Orthodox
3. Melkite Catholic
4. Armenian Orthodox
5. Armenian Catholic
6. Syriac Orthodox
7. Syriac Catholic
8. Roman Catholic
9. Chaldean
10. Assyrian
11. Coptic Orthodox
12. Coptic Catholic
13. Protestant (Evangelical)
14. Sunni Muslim (Hanafi)
15. Shia Muslim (Ja'fari)
16. Alawite
17. Ismaili
18. Druze
- Jewish (historic but minimal active presence)

## Implications for legal practice
- **Religion drives which court has jurisdiction** for the matter
- Each confession has its own substantive rules
- Mixed-confession marriages create complex jurisdictional questions
- Some Lebanese marry in civil-marriage countries (Cyprus, France) — these are recognized in LB civil registries but inheritance still governed by religion
- LB has **no civil personal-status option for nationals** — long-debated reform but unimplemented

## Christian personal status (general)
- Marriage in ecclesiastical court
- Divorce: limited grounds; varies by church (Maronite: very limited; Greek Orthodox: more grounds)
- Custody + alimony: courts apply church rules
- Inheritance: testator generally has more freedom

## Muslim personal status
- Sharia courts apply Islamic law (Sunni Hanafi for Sunni; Ja'fari for Shia)
- **Marriage**: Islamic marriage contract; dower (mahr)
- **Divorce**: talaq (husband-initiated), khula (wife-compensated), faskh (judicial)
- **Custody (Hadana)**: mother priority for young children; transitions to father (Sunni: ~7-9 years for boys, ~9-11 for girls; Ja'fari different)
- **Inheritance**: Quranic fixed shares (fara'id)

## Druze personal status
- Druze courts (separate from Sunni/Shia)
- Druze-specific rules on monogamy + divorce
- Inheritance: mostly testamentary freedom (unique among MENA Muslims)

## Inheritance summary
- Christians: more testamentary freedom; church rules may apply
- Sunni/Shia/Alawite/Ismaili: Sharia fixed shares; testament ≤ 1/3 of estate for non-heirs
- Druze: significant testamentary freedom

## Marriage outside religion
- **Civil marriage abroad** + LB registration recognized in civil registry
- Inheritance for such marriages: complex — often still governed by religion
- **Mixed-confession marriages**: must choose which court (Law 60 1936)
- **Inter-religious marriages**: governed by groom's religion (for inheritance)

## Practical drafting
- **Always confirm confession before drafting will / prenup / marriage doc**
- For non-religious clients: civil-marriage-abroad strategy + foreign will
- For inheritance: consider multiple coordinated documents per situs and applicable law

## Children + custody
- Best-interest standard increasingly applied
- Religious court determines initial custody per its rules
- Visitation enforcement: civil court mechanisms
