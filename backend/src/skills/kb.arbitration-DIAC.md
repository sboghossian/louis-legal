# DIAC (Dubai International Arbitration Centre) — Quick Reference

## Current rules
- DIAC Arbitration Rules 2022 (effective March 2022)
- Previous DIFC-LCIA Centre rules superseded after Decree 34/2021

## Key features
- Seat: by default Dubai (DIFC), unless parties agree otherwise
- Modern UNCITRAL Model Law alignment
- Single rules covering domestic and international
- Recognized worldwide under NY Convention (UAE acceded 2006)

## Common clauses

### Standard DIAC clause
> *Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this contract, including any question regarding its existence, validity or termination, shall be referred to and finally resolved by arbitration under the DIAC Rules, which Rules are deemed to be incorporated by reference into this clause. The seat of arbitration shall be Dubai (DIFC). The language of the arbitration shall be English. The Tribunal shall consist of [one/three] arbitrators.*

## Procedural milestones
- Request for Arbitration filed
- Response within 30 days
- Tribunal constituted (sole or three-arbitrator)
- Procedural order on timetable
- Statement of Claim → Statement of Defence → Reply → Rejoinder
- Hearings
- Award within 6 months from final brief (extendable)

## Costs
- Filing fee scales with amount in dispute
- Tribunal fees per scale
- Costs allocation in award (generally follows the event)

## Emergency arbitrator
- Available pre-tribunal constitution
- Decisions binding pending tribunal

## Joinder + consolidation
- Multi-party / multi-contract scenarios — see Articles 9-12

## Enforcement
- DIFC Courts as supervisory court for DIFC-seat arbitrations
- UAE federal recognition + enforcement (Federal Decree-Law 6/2018 on Arbitration)
- New York Convention enforcement abroad

## Choice of seat consequences
- DIFC seat → DIFC Courts supervisory
- Onshore Dubai seat → Dubai onshore courts supervisory
- Choice impacts curial procedure, language defaults, judicial review scope

## When to use
- UAE-related contracts where parties want neutrality + enforceability
- MENA-MENA cross-border
- International parties wanting Dubai's hub status
