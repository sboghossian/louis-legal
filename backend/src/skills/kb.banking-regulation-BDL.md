# BDL — Banque du Liban — Banking Regulation Quick Reference

## BDL overview
- **Banque du Liban** — Lebanon's central bank
- Established 1963
- Banking regulator + monetary authority
- Profoundly affected by 2019+ financial crisis

## Crisis context (2019+)
- Banking sector liquidity crisis since October 2019
- De facto capital controls (Circulars 158, 159, 161, etc.)
- Multiple exchange rates (official, Sayrafa, market)
- Bank Secrecy Law amendments tightened (2022)
- Ongoing IMF discussions for sectoral reform

## Regulated entities
- Commercial banks (~50 active)
- Specialized banks (housing, agricultural)
- Investment banks
- Money exchange
- Financial intermediation

## Banking license types
- **Commercial bank** — full activities
- **Specialized bank** — sector-specific
- **Investment bank** — securities-focused
- **Islamic banking window** (within commercial bank)
- **Branch of foreign bank** — limited

## Capital requirements
- Pre-crisis: LBP 250 billion minimum
- Post-crisis recapitalization requirements pending
- Significant haircut on existing equity discussed

## Prudential framework
- Pre-crisis: Basel III aligned
- Post-crisis: dependence on BDL emergency facilities
- Capital adequacy compromised across sector
- Cleanup expected per IMF framework

## Bank Secrecy Law
- Law 3/1956 — historic bank secrecy
- Lifted for AML investigations (Law 44/2015)
- 2022 amendments further reduced secrecy scope
- IMF expected to push further reforms

## AML/CFT
- Law 44/2015
- Special Investigation Commission (SIC) at BDL = FIU
- Reporting obligations on banks
- Customer Due Diligence requirements
- Sanctions screening

## Capital controls (de facto)
- Withdrawal limits per BDL Circulars
- Differential rates for USD vs LBP withdrawals
- "Lollar" (haircut USD) for foreign deposits
- Restrictions on transfer abroad

## Currency system (multi-tier)
- Official rate (1,507 LBP/USD historically; later devalued)
- Sayrafa platform rate
- Free-market rate (parallel)
- Multiple rates for different uses (taxes, salaries, certain payments)

## Recent reforms / discussion
- Banking sector restructuring per IMF Staff Level Agreement (in negotiation)
- Capital control formalization (capital control law pending)
- Banking sector recapitalization framework
- Resolution framework for distressed banks

## Practical considerations
- Heightened compliance scrutiny for Lebanese banking activities
- Cross-border transactions: enhanced due diligence required
- Lebanese-bank-issued documents may receive enhanced scrutiny internationally
- Significant uncertainty around resolution outcomes

## Outlook
- Banking sector reform tied to IMF program
- 2025-2026 expected milestone for substantial reform
- Major changes likely to come from any agreement
