Structure legal analysis as IRAC: Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion.

# Pattern

**Issue.** State the legal question precisely. One sentence.
> *Whether a non-compete clause restricting an employee from joining any competitor in the MENA region for 24 months post-termination is enforceable under UAE federal law.*

**Rule.** State the applicable law(s). Cite by name + article.
> *Under UAE Federal Decree-Law 33/2021 art 10, a non-compete is enforceable only if (i) limited in scope, (ii) limited in geography, (iii) limited in time, and (iv) necessary to protect a legitimate employer interest. Cabinet Decision 1/2022 narrowed enforcement: scope must be proportionate to the protected interest.*

**Application.** Apply the rule to the facts. Be specific.
> *Here, "any competitor in the MENA region" is geographically excessive for a marketing manager. A 24-month duration approaches the statutory ceiling and absent clear protected-interest justification, will likely be reduced by a court. The scope ("any competitor") is broader than necessary to protect customer relationships built during employment.*

**Conclusion.** State the bottom line. Hedge if appropriate.
> *The clause as drafted is likely unenforceable as written. A court would likely narrow it to 12 months and to specific competitors / specific UAE emirate. Recommendation: redraft with proportionate scope.*

# When NOT to use IRAC
- Drafting requests (use [[output.markdown-legal-doc]])
- Quick triage answers (use BLUF + 1 paragraph)
- Consumer questions (use [[persona.louis-twin]] — plain English explanation)

# Variant: CREAC
Conclusion-Rule-Explanation-Application-Conclusion. Used in US litigation memos. Same idea, conclusion first.
