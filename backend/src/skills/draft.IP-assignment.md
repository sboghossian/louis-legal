Draft an Intellectual Property Assignment Agreement transferring ownership of IP from Assignor to Assignee.

# Required inputs
1. Assignor (current owner) + Assignee (recipient)
2. Description of IP (list of patents, applications, trademarks, copyrights, designs, trade secrets, code)
3. Consideration (cash, equity, services, or "good and valuable consideration receipt acknowledged")
4. Effective date
5. Warranties (Assignor has clear title, no encumbrances)

# Structure
1. Recitals — purpose of assignment
2. Assignment of IP (full, irrevocable, including all rights to sue for past infringement)
3. List of IP (annexed schedule)
4. Further assurances — Assignor agrees to sign documents needed to perfect Assignee's title
5. Reps & warranties: Assignor's clear title, no encumbrances, no third-party rights, no pending challenges
6. Indemnification for warranty breaches
7. Confidentiality
8. Power of attorney — Assignee may sign on Assignor's behalf for registration/perfection
9. Governing law (lex loci of IP for registration matters)

# Critical
- **Moral rights** — civil-law systems recognize inalienable moral rights of authors; assignment cannot transfer attribution and integrity rights. Address explicitly: waiver where possible; consent to specific uses.
- **Future IP** — assignment must clearly cover both existing AND future IP if applicable (common in employment / consulting contexts)
- **Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction perfection** — patents and trademarks require local registration changes; assignment alone doesn't update registry
- **Tax** — IP assignment may trigger capital gains, VAT, or transfer pricing scrutiny

# Use cases
- Employee → Employer (foundational for IP-heavy businesses)
- Founder → Newco (incorporation cleanup)
- Consultant → Client (output of services)
- Acquired entity → Acquirer (M&A)
