Draft a Power of Attorney (PoA / وكالة).

# Types
1. **General PoA** — broad powers; many jurisdictions narrowly construe; some prohibit for real estate or marriage
2. **Special / Limited PoA** — specific acts (sell property X, sign contract Y)
3. **Durable PoA** — survives principal's incapacity (common-law concept; civil-law analogs vary)
4. **Springing PoA** — activates on a condition (often incapacity)

# Required inputs
- Principal: full identification (ID + civil status)
- Attorney-in-fact / agent: full identification
- Scope: precise enumeration of powers (broad PoAs are often weakly enforced)
- Duration: from-to dates, or until revoked
- Jurisdiction: where the PoA will be used
- Use country: where the agent will exercise (may need apostille / consular legalization)

# Form requirements (vary heavily by jurisdiction)
- **LB**: notarized; for use abroad, apostille via the Notary Public + Ministry of Foreign Affairs
- **KSA**: notarized via Saudi Notary Public OR Saudi embassy abroad; specific format required by the Sharia courts and government bodies
- **UAE**: notarized via Dubai Courts Notary Public OR Abu Dhabi Judicial Department; for use abroad, MOFA attestation
- **EU/UK**: apostille under Hague Convention

# Powers commonly enumerated
- Sign contracts on principal's behalf
- Represent in court (if jurisdiction allows non-lawyer representation — usually no)
- Buy / sell / lease real estate (often must be specifically enumerated)
- Open / close / operate bank accounts (banks often require their own form)
- Vote shares in companies
- Sign tax returns
- Customs and import / export
- Marriage / divorce (very restricted; many jurisdictions prohibit PoA for personal-status acts)
- Legalize documents

# Revocation
- Principal may revoke at any time (unless coupled with interest / irrevocable PoA)
- Notice of revocation must be given to the agent AND any third party relying on the PoA

# Bilingual
LB/UAE/KSA: Arabic required for local use; English / French may be added for international use.

# Critical
Surface to user: PoAs to be used internationally typically need apostille or consular legalization. The chain is: notary → MFA → consulate.
