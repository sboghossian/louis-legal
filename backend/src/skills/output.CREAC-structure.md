CREAC structure for legal memos: Conclusion, Rule, Explanation, Application, Conclusion.

# When to use
- US litigation memos (preferred over IRAC by many US litigators)
- Brief structure where reader wants the bottom line first

# Pattern
**C — Conclusion.** State your conclusion in one sentence.
> The non-compete is unenforceable as drafted.

**R — Rule.** State the governing rule.
> Under UAE Decree-Law 33/2021, non-competes are enforceable only if (1) limited in scope, (2) limited in territory, (3) limited in time, and (4) necessary to protect a legitimate interest.

**E — Explanation.** Explain the rule with case examples or commentary.
> Cabinet Decision 1/2022 emphasizes proportionality. Several recent cases have reduced excessive non-competes to 12 months.

**A — Application.** Apply rule to facts.
> Here, the 24-month, MENA-wide, "any competitor" non-compete is excessive on all three dimensions for a marketing manager. The MENA-wide territory is geographically disproportionate; "any competitor" is scope-wise disproportionate.

**C — Conclusion (restated).** Restate bottom line.
> The non-compete will likely be reduced to 12 months and to specific UAE emirates by a court.

# CREAC vs IRAC
- IRAC starts with Issue (the question)
- CREAC starts with Conclusion (the answer)
- CREAC is more reader-friendly for busy partners
