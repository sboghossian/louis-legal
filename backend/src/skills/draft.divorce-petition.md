Draft a divorce petition. **Personal-status law is the most fragmented area** — religion/confession + jurisdiction together determine which court and what rules.

# Required inputs
- Petitioner + Respondent
- Marriage details (date, place, registry, confession/civil)
- Children (names, ages, residence)
- Grounds for divorce (jurisdiction-specific)
- Petitioner's residence and Respondent's whereabouts
- Religion / confession of parties

# Jurisdictional + religious matrix

## Lebanon — 18 confessional courts
- Sunni / Shia / Druze → Sharia courts; talaq, khula, judicial divorce
- Christian confessions → ecclesiastical courts (Maronite, Greek Orthodox, Catholic, Armenian, etc.)
- Civil → no civil divorce mechanism for Lebanese; civil-married Lebanese resort to foreign civil courts
- Mixed-confession marriages have additional complexity

## KSA — Sharia courts
- Talaq (husband's pronouncement)
- Khula (wife's compensated divorce)
- Judicial divorce (faskh) on specific grounds

## UAE
- Muslims → Sharia courts
- Non-Muslims → Federal Law 41/2022 civil-law personal-status (Abu Dhabi pioneering)
- DIFC Wills + Probate registry deals with succession (separate)

## EU member states
- Civil law; no-fault divorce widely available
- Brussels IIa Regulation on cross-border jurisdiction

# Petition structure
1. Court / tribunal identification
2. Parties (with residency / nationality)
3. Marriage details (date, type, witnesses)
4. Children (names, ages, current residence)
5. Grounds for divorce (statute / Sharia / canonical citations)
6. Property situation (assets, debts, division sought)
7. Custody arrangement sought
8. Child support / spousal maintenance sought
9. Interim relief (residence, financial support pendente lite)
10. Final relief sought
11. Annexed documents (marriage certificate, ID, financial disclosure)
12. Service on Respondent

# Critical
- **Jurisdiction first** — Petitioner's habitual residence + Respondent's domicile + child's habitual residence — multi-jurisdictional cases need conflict-of-laws analysis
- **Religion of marriage** ≠ religion of divorce in some jurisdictions
- **Foreign divorce recognition** — Lebanese courts may not recognize foreign-civil divorces of LB nationals
- **Notarized documents** + apostille for cross-border use

This is a high-stakes drafting area requiring local counsel — always recommend that.
