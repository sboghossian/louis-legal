Draft a Law Firm Engagement Letter — the foundational document for legal services to a client.

# Required inputs
- Firm + Client
- Scope of representation
- Fee structure (hourly / fixed / contingency / hybrid)
- Conflict-check confirmation
- Engagement effective date

# Structure
1. Identification of parties (firm + named client)
2. Scope of engagement — precise matter description; what's in/out of scope
3. Fee structure
   - Hourly: rates by seniority + billing increments + estimate
   - Fixed: amount + scope locked
   - Contingency: % + expense responsibility
   - Hybrid: blended
4. Expenses — pass-through, third-party costs, travel
5. Billing cadence — monthly / on completion / on milestones
6. Retainer / advance — if required, refundable balance treatment
7. Conflict-of-interest acknowledgment — Firm has run conflict check; Client agrees to disclose new conflicts
8. Confidentiality — bar rules apply; Firm respects
9. File retention — duration, return/destruction at end
10. Termination — either side; fees due to date of termination
11. Mandatory arbitration / mediation of fee disputes (optional)
12. Governing law

# Critical (bar-rule compliance)
- **Fee transparency** — most bars require written engagement letters for fees over a threshold
- **Conflict check** — confirm done before sending the letter
- **Trust account** for retainers — most bars require segregated client funds
- **Termination rights** — Client always has right to terminate; Firm's right is limited per bar rules
- **Reasonable fee** — fees subject to bar review for unconscionability

# Anti-patterns
- Vague scope ("legal advice as needed") — invites scope-creep disputes
- Hourly without estimate — many bars require ballpark
- Contingency without explicit court approval where required (some MENA jurisdictions)

# Jurisdictional notes
- **LB**: bar rules on contingency fees; cap on percentage in some matters
- **KSA**: contingency fees disfavoured for many matters; declarable to MOJ
- **UAE / DIFC / ADGM**: greater fee flexibility in financial centres
- **US states**: each state bar has specific rules (e.g., NY 1200.1; CA Rule 1.5)
