Draft a Distribution Agreement appointing a distributor for specified products in a defined territory.

# Required inputs
1. Supplier / Manufacturer + Distributor
2. Products (precise scope; product changes notice requirements)
3. Territory (exclusive / non-exclusive / sole)
4. Term
5. Pricing (wholesale price; price-list update mechanism)
6. Minimum purchase commitments (MPC) per quarter / year

# Exclusivity options
- **Exclusive**: only this Distributor in territory; Supplier may not sell directly
- **Sole**: only this Distributor third-party in territory, but Supplier may sell directly
- **Non-exclusive**: Supplier may appoint other Distributors

# Standard clauses
1. Appointment + territory + exclusivity
2. Product scope + modification rights
3. Minimum purchase / sales targets + consequences of miss
4. Pricing + payment terms (LC / TT / prepayment)
5. Marketing obligations on Distributor (spend %, channels, trade shows)
6. Distributor's reporting obligations
7. IP & trademarks (Distributor uses Supplier's marks under license; quality control)
8. Confidentiality
9. Limitation of liability (consequential damages waiver typical)
10. Term + termination (typical: convenience with long notice; cause with cure)
11. Post-termination: customer transition; inventory buyback; non-compete

# Critical jurisdictional issues
- **MENA commercial agencies acts** — Many jurisdictions (UAE, KSA, Egypt, Qatar) protect "commercial agents" with statutory indemnity on non-renewal IF registered. Determine whether the relationship is a "distributor" (sale-and-resale) or "agent" (selling on behalf of) — registered agency creates large termination indemnity risk.
- **EU competition law** — block exemption for vertical restraints (Reg 2022/720); price-fixing prohibitions; passive-sales requirements for exclusive territories
- **KSA** — Commercial Agencies Law requires Saudi national or 51%-Saudi company; foreign suppliers cannot appoint non-Saudi sole distributors in many product categories

# Anti-trap
Do NOT use "Agent" language in a distribution agreement if you don't want commercial-agency consequences. Distributors buy and resell; agents solicit orders on behalf of the principal.
