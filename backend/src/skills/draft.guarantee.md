Draft a Guarantee — guarantor undertakes to satisfy obligations of principal debtor.

# Two structures
1. **Demand guarantee** (autonomous, on-first-demand): guarantor pays on demand without proof of underlying default
2. **Conditional guarantee** (surety): guarantor liable only on actual default by principal debtor, after demand on debtor

# Required inputs
- Guarantor (and entity confirmation)
- Beneficiary (party in whose favour)
- Principal (debtor whose obligations guaranteed)
- Underlying obligation reference
- Maximum liability amount (cap)
- Duration / expiry
- Demand mechanics

# Standard sections
1. Recitals — identify the underlying transaction
2. Guarantee declaration — guarantor unconditionally undertakes payment
3. Payment mechanics — on first written demand / on certified default
4. Maximum liability — capped amount (almost always negotiated)
5. Expiry / release — fixed date or specific event
6. Continuing guarantee or one-shot
7. Waivers (notice, defenses, subrogation timing)
8. Counter-indemnity from Principal to Guarantor (separate document)
9. Set-off rights / restrictions
10. Governing law

# Jurisdictional notes
- **KSA** — Sharia: guarantees must be specific; suretyship not "abstract" or commercial-paper-style; demand guarantees disfavoured in pure commercial context (use kafala structures)
- **UAE federal** — commercial guarantees per Federal Law 18/1993 (Commercial Code); demand guarantees common in trade finance
- **DIFC / ADGM** — full common-law UK-style demand guarantees
- **LB** — civil-law caution / aval distinctions; suretyship subject to civil code rules

# Critical
- **Independent vs accessory** — independent (on-demand) guarantees are difficult to defend; surety guarantees allow underlying-contract defences
- **Personal vs corporate** — for personal guarantees, ensure separate spousal/asset considerations
- **Letter of Credit vs Guarantee** — different instruments with different rules; LCs governed by UCP 600/ISP 98
