Draft a DMCA-compliant (or equivalent) takedown notice for infringing online content.

# DMCA (US — 17 USC 512(c)(3))
Required elements:
1. Physical or electronic signature of authorized person
2. Identification of copyrighted work claimed infringed
3. Identification of infringing material with sufficient detail to locate
4. Contact information (address, phone, email)
5. Statement under penalty of perjury that complainant has good-faith belief use is not authorized
6. Statement that information is accurate and complainant is authorized

# Pattern
```
To: [Designated DMCA Agent, Platform]
Date:

I am [name], acting on behalf of [rights-holder] which owns the copyright to [work].

The following content infringes our copyright:
- URL: [exact URL]
- Description: [content + how it infringes]

The above use is not authorized by the rights-holder, its agent, or the law.

I have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized.

The information in this notice is accurate, and under penalty of perjury, I am authorized to act on behalf of the owner.

[Signature, contact details]
```

# Non-US equivalents
- **EU DSA + national copyright laws** — notice-and-action mechanisms (Article 16 DSA)
- **UK** — Copyright, Designs and Patents Act + notice frameworks
- **MENA** — typically platform-specific procedures + court-orders for non-compliant hosts
- **KSA** — Anti-Cybercrime Law + Copyright Law; can escalate to CITC if platform unresponsive

# After takedown
- Keep records: dates, URLs, screenshots
- Monitor for re-uploads
- For repeated infringement: pattern of abuse may justify subpoena for poster identity (US 17 USC 512(h))
- Trademark infringement is separate — use platform-specific TM forms or cease-and-desist (see [[draft.cease-and-desist]])

# Counter-notice handling
If the alleged infringer counter-notices, platform may restore content unless rights-holder files suit within 10-14 days (DMCA window).
