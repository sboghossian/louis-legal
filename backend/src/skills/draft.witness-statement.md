Draft a witness statement — first-person factual narrative for court / arbitration.

# Required inputs
- Witness identification
- Subject of dispute
- Relevant time period
- Facts witness can personally attest to
- Document references

# Standard structure (common-law style)
1. **Heading**: court / arbitral tribunal name, case number
2. **Witness identification**: full name, address, occupation, capacity in which testifying
3. **Statement of truth**: "I make this statement believing the facts stated to be true"
4. **Statement of personal knowledge**: "The facts in this statement are within my personal knowledge except where stated"
5. **Narrative** — chronological, first-person, factual
6. **Document references** — exhibits attached
7. **Signature + date**

# Drafting principles
- **First person, narrative** — "I attended the meeting on [date]…", not "The witness states…"
- **Personal knowledge only** — distinguish what witnessed vs hearsay (some hearsay admissible in some forums)
- **Chronological** — date-anchored sequence
- **Specific facts** — dates, times, places, persons, words spoken
- **Avoid opinion** unless witness is expert (separate type of statement)
- **Avoid argument** — let counsel argue from facts
- **Document references** — cite exhibits by number

# Jurisdictional variations
- **Common-law (DIFC, ADGM, UK, US)**: witness statement is the primary direct evidence; cross-examination at hearing
- **Civil-law (LB, KSA, UAE-onshore, FR)**: witness testimony at hearing rather than pre-written statement; written declarations have less weight
- **International arbitration**: IBA Rules on Evidence accommodate both styles; written statement + cross-examination common

# Critical
- **Sign + date** the statement
- **Attach a Statement of Truth** (required in most common-law jurisdictions)
- **Don't have lawyer ghostwrite** — must be in witness's voice and knowledge
- **Specific facts** > generalizations
- **Address counter-narratives** — proactively address adverse facts
