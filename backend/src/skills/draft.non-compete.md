Draft a non-compete clause. Enforceability varies massively by jurisdiction.

# Inputs
- Restrained party (employee / departing shareholder / departing partner)
- Restraining party
- Duration (12-24 months typical max enforceable)
- Geographic scope (country, region, named cities)
- Activity scope (named industry, named competitors, customer-facing roles only)
- Consideration (often required — see below)
- Governing law

# Jurisdictional enforceability
- **LB**: Enforceable post-employment if (i) protects legitimate interest, (ii) limited scope, (iii) limited geography, (iv) limited duration; consideration recommended though not strictly required. Courts narrowly construe.
- **KSA**: Enforceable up to 2 years with reasonable scope (Labor Law Art 83); excessive scope ⇒ judicial narrowing.
- **UAE federal**: Decree-Law 33/2021 Art 10 + Cabinet Decision 1/2022 — enforceable up to 2 years, must be proportionate, may require court order.
- **DIFC**: Enforceable per DIFC Employment Law Art 13 — reasonable scope test; common-law principles apply.
- **France**: Strict — requires written form, time-limited, geographically limited, activity-limited, AND mandatory compensation (typically 25-30% of salary for the restraint duration).
- **California** (if relevant): essentially unenforceable for employees.

# Standard clause structure
> *During the Term and for a period of [12-24] months thereafter, the Employee shall not, within [defined geography], directly or indirectly engage in [defined activity] in competition with the Employer's business of [defined scope], whether as employee, consultant, owner, or otherwise.*

# Carve-outs
- Investment in publicly listed companies (typically up to 1-5% ownership permitted)
- Non-competitive employment in same industry
- Return to home jurisdiction (if employee was relocated)

# Pair with non-solicit
Non-solicit clauses (customers, employees) are easier to enforce than non-competes. Use both. See [[draft.non-solicit]].
