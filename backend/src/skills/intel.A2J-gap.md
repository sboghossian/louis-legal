Access to Justice (A2J) gap — the foundational case for consumer legal AI.

# Headline
- **~92% of low-income legal needs go unmet globally** (Legal Services Corporation 2017 + 2022 updates)
- Comparable gap in MENA: estimated 80-95% depending on country + issue type

# Issue types affected
- Family law (divorce, custody, support)
- Housing (eviction, landlord disputes)
- Consumer (debt, credit, fraud)
- Employment (wrongful termination, wage theft)
- Immigration (status, work permits)
- Criminal defense (low-tier offenses, plea bargaining)

# Causes
- **Cost**: $352/hour avg unaffordable for most consumers
- **Geographic access**: rural areas, smaller cities lack lawyers
- **Language barriers**: bilingual / minority languages
- **Time / process complexity**: legal system hard to navigate solo
- **Cultural barriers**: stigma around legal involvement

# Existing solutions
- **Legal Aid** — underfunded; can only serve fraction of need
- **Pro bono** — volunteer effort; capacity-limited
- **Self-help books / websites** — passive info, no personalization
- **AI legal assistants (new)** — Louis Twin + competitors

# Louis Twin opportunity
- **Affordable scale** — AI marginal cost is near-zero
- **Multi-language** — Arabic, French, English natively
- **24/7 availability**
- **Plain English** — translates jargon, walks through process
- **Lawyer referral** — when matters require legal practice, refer

# Bar-rule constraint
- Cannot replace lawyer entirely (UPL concerns)
- Position as legal **information** + **orientation**, not advice
- Refer + handoff when professional needed

# Tab metrics
- Net consumer benefit: legal information access for ~5 billion currently underserved
- Long-tail revenue: small ARPU × very large user base
- Mission alignment with bar associations / legal aid orgs
