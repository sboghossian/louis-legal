Review indemnification clauses for balance, scope, and procedural fairness.

# Key dimensions to inspect

1. **Scope** — what triggers the indemnity?
   - Third-party claims only? Or broader (including first-party losses)?
   - Breach of warranty / covenant / law / IP / data?
   - Negligence / willful misconduct?

2. **Asymmetry**
   - Does each Party indemnify proportionally to risk?
   - Common balanced setup: Provider indemnifies IP / data-breach / willful misconduct; Client indemnifies its content / use violations / third-party claims caused by Client

3. **Procedure (Defense)**
   - Notice timing (prompt notice usually required; failure consequences clear)
   - Control of defense (Indemnifying Party usually controls; with exceptions for reputation-critical matters)
   - Settlement consent (Indemnified Party consent required if settlement admits liability or affects its rights)
   - Cooperation obligations on Indemnified Party

4. **Caps / Carve-outs**
   - Most indemnities are uncapped or carve out from the general liability cap — verify alignment
   - IP indemnity is most commonly carved out (so it doesn't reduce general damages cap)
   - Data-breach indemnity is increasingly carved out

5. **Time limits**
   - Most indemnities survive the agreement for some period
   - Common: 12-36 months for general; longer (5-7 years or statute of limitations) for IP, data, tax

# Red flags
- Indemnity covers Indemnified Party's own negligence (rare but happens — often unenforceable as against public policy)
- "Defend and indemnify" without notice procedure or cooperation requirements
- Settlement consent missing — Indemnifying Party could settle in a way that prejudices Indemnified Party
- No cap on Indemnified Party's contribution claims back

# Output
Per indemnity clause: rate the **balance** (1-5) and recommend rebalance positions.
