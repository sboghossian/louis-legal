# KSA Family Law — Quick Reference

## Source
- Sharia law (Sunni Hanbali school as default for KSA Muslims)
- Personal Status Law (Royal Decree 73/1443H 2022) — first codified personal status law in KSA

## Key changes from 2022 codification
- Reduces judicial discretion (previously each judge interpreted Sharia)
- Clearer rules on marriage, divorce, custody, inheritance
- Maintains Sharia framework
- Applies to Saudi nationals + foreign Muslims by default
- Non-Muslims may choose foreign-law application in certain matters

## Marriage
- Requires:
  - Mutual consent
  - Mahr (dower) — gift from husband to wife
  - Two adult Muslim male witnesses (or one male + two female under traditional rules)
  - Wife's guardian (wali) — typically father / male relative
  - Registration with court

## Divorce
- **Talaq** — husband-initiated; new law requires court documentation
- **Khula** — wife compensates husband to end marriage
- **Faskh** — judicial dissolution on specific grounds (harm, non-support, impotence, etc.)
- **Mutual divorce** — both consent

### Iddah period
- Waiting period before remarriage: typically 3 menstrual cycles
- Pregnancy delays remarriage until birth

## Custody (Hadana)
- Mother priority for young children
- Transitions to father at certain ages (boys ~9-10, girls at marriage in some interpretations)
- 2022 law allows mother extended custody under best-interest standard

## Maintenance (Nafaqa)
- Husband must provide maintenance during marriage
- Continues during iddah after divorce
- Child support continues until child is independent (males: until adulthood; females: until marriage)

## Inheritance
- Quranic fixed shares (fara'id):
  - Male heir typically receives twice female heir's share (same class)
  - Spouse share varies based on presence of children
  - No inheritance to/from non-Muslim relatives (debated)
- Testament limited to 1/3 of estate for non-heirs
- Cannot bequeath to heirs in addition to fara'id share

## Non-Muslims in KSA
- 2022 personal status law allows foreign-law option in certain matters
- DIFC-style alternatives not available (KSA has no equivalent free-zone option)
- Cross-border marriages + divorces: case-by-case recognition

## Practice notes
- Strict procedural requirements: court-supervised documentation
- Lawyer representation increasingly important
- Specialized family courts established under judicial reform
